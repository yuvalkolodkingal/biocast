# Split-mould negatives — design record, verification, and print settings

Generated by `biocast/mould.py` from the validated `biocast` engine. Two typologies:
the **hollow ovoid vessel** (the paper's proven Fig. 6–7 typology, 6 parts) and the
**hollow-core block** (2 parts). All lengths mm.

Selected designs, scored with the existing estimator before any mould work:

| design | d_max | cure | wall / web | cemented frac | score (5–95 %) | limiting mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| vessel `S_cure` | 4.0 mm | 28 d | wall 26 mm | 1.000 | 0.743 (0.504–0.938) | castability |
| block `B_split` | 4.0 mm | 28 d | web 30 mm | 0.993 | 0.762 (0.000–0.998) | aeration |

Both sit on the **28-day-cure** branch of the sieve-vs-cure trade, not the 14-day
branch. At d_max = 4 mm with a 14-day 90 % RH cure there is no feasible wall
thickness (jamming wants ≥ 24 mm, drainage allows ≤ 21 mm); the 28-day cure moves
L_dry from 10.5 mm to 21.0 mm and opens wall ≤ 42 mm. The vessel's 26 mm wall and
the block's 30 mm web are both inside that window. **The moulds below are only
castable on the 28-day schedule.** A 14-day cure requires sieving to d_max = 3 mm,
which the aperture table re-checks separately.

---

## 1. Parting plane

### Vessel: the widest section is NOT the equator

The ovoid taper (`ovoid = 0.28`) moves the maximum cross-section off centre.
Measured on the voxel grid and confirmed against the analytic profile:

| | z (mm) | silhouette radius (mm) |
|---|---|---|
| true widest section | **−21.0** (analytic −20.13) | **50.04** |
| equator (the naive default) | 0.0 | 48.82 |

Parting at the equator would leave a **1.2 mm re-entrant lip** — material that has
to scrape past the mould wall on the way out. On a green bio-cement body that is a
spalled edge, and it is invisible in a render. The parting plane is therefore placed
at the computed maximum, `widest_section()`, not assumed. Verified afterwards:
re-entrance **0.0 mm²**, and the silhouette radius is monotone non-increasing away
from the parting plane in both directions (max re-entrant step 0.000 mm each way).

### Block: every section ties, so the tiebreak is draw depth

A prism has no unique widest section — the plateau is 174 mm wide out of a 190 mm
extent, and `widest_section()` reports `is_prismatic = True`. Width cannot choose
the plane, so the other cost decides: draft relief is proportional to draw depth, so
the plane goes at **z = 0**, mid-height. Draw depth **95 mm** per half instead of
190 mm if parted at an end face — halving the draw halves the relief the draft costs.

## 2. Draft angle

| | draft | rationale |
|---|---|---|
| vessel | **2.5°** | fragile green body, deep draw (98 mm from parting to the far pole) |
| block | **1.5°** | keeps relief inside the ASTM C90 ±3.2 mm dimensional tolerance |

Draft is applied as a **directional cone sweep of the field**, never by rescaling the
object — the object is the validated design, and shrinking it would silently
invalidate the wall thickness that the drying and oxygen models depend on.

The direction is not a free choice. For a fixed core of radius `B(z)` inside a body
whose hole is `R_hole(z)`, withdrawing toward +z requires

```
B(z) <= min over z' <= z of R_hole(z')
```

and a running minimum from below is non-increasing. Three consequences, each of
which changes the part that gets printed:

1. **An integral core boss can never widen toward the parting plane.** The only
   drafted solution narrows toward it. The block's cores therefore come out as an
   **hourglass** (widest at the outer faces, waist at mid-height), not the single
   monotonic taper `grammars.block` applies for a one-piece bottom-draw mould — in
   a split mould that taper has the wrong sense in the lower half. Verified: boss
   cross-section 75 008 mm² at the outer face → 32 736 mm² at the parting plane
   (lower half), 32 992 → 75 264 mm² (upper), i.e. narrowing toward the parting
   plane on both sides.
2. **The vessel cannot use an integral boss at all.** Its internal cavity is ~45 mm
   across at the parting plane but the top aperture bore is only Ø32, so the running
   minimum pins a boss at Ø32 — it would leave the cavity unformed and cast a 32 mm
   wall where 26 mm was designed. The vessel gets **two loose cores** instead, one
   per half, each withdrawn upward through the open parting face.
3. **Where the form already has more natural draft than θ, the sweep is the
   identity.** The correction is confined to the flat band around the parting plane:
   the vessel's cavity is oversize by only **593.8 mm³ (0.069 % of the form)**.

Realised draft, measured back off the generated cavity: lower half **1.74°**, upper
half **3.09°** (2nd-percentile of the local wall angle; both ≥ the 2.5° request where
the form itself is flat, higher where the form's own curvature contributes).

### Block: relief lands on the faces, not the web

The block's cavity is generated by **eroding away from the parting plane**, so the
nominal section is preserved *at* the parting plane and the relief is taken off the
outer faces. Relief is 2.49 mm/face predicted, 2.0 mm/face realised (voxel-quantised
at 2.0 mm pitch), i.e. a 4 mm total reduction on each of the web and the face shell.

Sections measured on the mid-plane row, with the **nominal** body measured on the same
grid so the draft cost is separated from the geometry the design already had:

| location | nominal fs / web | as-cast fs / web | draft cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| parting plane (z = 0) | 38 / 32 mm | 38 / 32 mm | 0 / 0 mm |
| prismatic band (\|z\| ≤ 87 mm) | 38 / 32 mm | **34 / 28 mm** | 4 / 4 mm |
| last 8 mm at each end (\|z\| → 95) | 22 / 16 mm | 22 / 16 mm | **0 / 0 mm** |
| **ASTM C90 minimum** | — | **32 / 25 mm** | — |

Nominal spec is face shell 37 mm / web 30 mm, raised from the standard 32/25
*specifically* to absorb the draft; the 38/32 measured values are that spec rounded up
to the 2.0 mm voxel grid. At 32/25 nominal the as-cast web would fall to ~20 mm, below
both the C90 floor and the 3 × d_max = 12 mm packing minimum. Void fraction 44.8 %,
inside the 40–50 % CMU band.

**Read the third row carefully — it is a real limitation, not a rounding artefact.**
Over the prismatic body the as-cast section is 34/28 mm and clears the C90 floors. But
in the last ~8 mm at each end the section falls to 22/16 mm, and **16 mm is below the
25 mm web floor**. That reduction is *not* caused by draft: the nominal body measures
the same 22/16 there (draft cost 0.0 mm), because it is the block's own **8 mm edge
fillet** rolling off — the fillet the team's own rule requires (r ≥ 1.5 × d_max = 6 mm)
to keep a sharp corner from initiating a crack. So the thin end zone is a deliberate
feature of the cast object inherited from the validated design, and the mould
reproduces it faithfully; the draft adds nothing to it.

It still needs a decision before anyone loads this mould, because C90 measures web
thickness at its thinnest point and this block would not pass if measured through the
filleted end zone:

- If the unit must be **C90-compliant as measured anywhere**, reduce `fillet_r` on the
  block from 8 mm to ~6 mm (still ≥ 1.5 × d_max) and/or raise nominal web further, then
  re-run — the fillet depth, not the draft, is the parameter to move.
- If the unit is a **non-structural / demonstrator cast** (which is what the paper's
  artefacts are), the filleted end zone is acceptable and the governing number is the
  34/28 mm prismatic section.

This is flagged rather than papered over because the two readings lead to different
mould geometry, and the choice is the team's, not the generator's.

## 3. Registration keys

Truncated cones (dowel on the lower half, socket in the upper), **3 per mould**,
asymmetrically placed so the halves mate one way only.

| | vessel | block |
|---|---|---|
| base Ø / top Ø / height | 18 / 12 / 9 mm | 22 / 15 / 11 mm |
| socket clearance | 0.30 mm | 0.30 mm |
| placement | circular ring, r = 78.8 mm | rectangular perimeter |
| angles | 0°, 130°, 215° | 30°, 150°, 275° |
| key volume, actual vs nominal | 4 676 / 4 835 mm³ | 9 472 / 8 976 mm³ |

The cones are 12–22 mm across at the base — far above the ~3–4 mm feature size where
FDM detail breaks down, and they take the shear from clamping rather than the bolts.
The 0.30 mm socket clearance is the printed-fit allowance; it is the only place in
the mould where a sub-millimetre dimension is intentional.

**Chirality had to be tested twice, on two different symmetry groups.** A circular
flange has the full O(2), so the gap-sequence test (`keys_are_chiral`) applies. A
rectangular flange has only {identity, 180°, two mirrors}, and an angular pattern
that is chiral on a circle can become symmetric once projected onto the rectangle —
the block's first candidate set (0°, 118°, 242°) projected to a **mirror-symmetric**
triple, which would have let the halves mate flipped. `keys_are_chiral_xy` tests the
explicit xy layout against the rectangle's own group. Final: both moulds **chiral**,
minimum key-pair separation 319.5 mm on the block.

A related failure was silent and worth recording: a circular key ring sized for the
block's long side put the keys at y = 197 mm when the flange only reaches y = 143 mm.
Every key landed in air, the intersection with the half deleted them, and the mould
would have printed **with no registration at all** while every volume check still
closed — a feature that was never there cannot unbalance anything. Hence
`ring_positions_rect` and the actual-vs-nominal key volume check in the table above.

## 4. Vents, feed gates, and drains — the jamming criterion cuts BOTH ways

This is the part most likely to be got wrong, because the same criterion implies
**opposite** sizing for two classes of aperture.

**Aggregate passages** must clear the bridging limit or the mix jams and starves the
feature (Zuriguel 2005: R_c = 4.94 for spheres, 6.0 for angular grains; Vani 2022:
always clogs below W/d = 3). At d_max = 4 mm:

```
6 x d_max = 6 x 4 mm = 24 mm    <- every feed and vent passage must exceed this
```

**Liquid-only drains must do the opposite.** They have to pass the spent cementation
solution while *retaining* the aggregate, so they are sized deliberately *below* the
certain-clog ratio and bridge by design:

```
3 x d_max = 3 x 4 mm = 12 mm    <- drains must stay UNDER this
```

Sizing a drain at 24 mm does not make a better drain; it makes a hole the mix falls
out of. `check_apertures` classifies each aperture and applies the matching test.

| aperture | class | clear | ratio to d_max | test | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| feed / vent gate, ×2 vessel, ×4 block | aggregate | 26.0 mm | 6.50 | ≥ 24 mm | pass |
| open parting face (vessel) | aggregate | 100.1 mm | 25.02 | ≥ 24 mm | pass |
| vessel top bore (cast by the loose core) | aggregate | 32.0 mm | 8.00 | ≥ 24 mm | pass |
| block core opening, as-cast waist | aggregate | 114 mm | 28.50 | ≥ 24 mm | pass |
| liquid drain, ×6 vessel / ×8 block | liquid | 6.0 mm | 1.50 | ≤ 12 mm | pass |

At d_max = 3 mm (the sieved branch) every aggregate aperture still passes with more
margin (6 × 3 = 18 mm), and the 6 mm drains remain under 3 × 3 = 9 mm. The mould
geometry is valid on **both** branches of the sieve-vs-cure trade; only the cast
body's wall thickness depends on which branch is taken.

The **largest** aperture is the open parting face itself — 100 mm across at the
widest section, which is the whole point of the split-mould technique. It is the feed
path and the oxygen path at once, and the reason a 26 mm gate is enough: the gates
only handle top-up, not primary filling.

## 5. Mould wall thickness

**20 mm on both moulds.** Chosen on stiffness, not on print time. Design pressure
from the wet mix:

```
rho = 1890 kg/m3, depth 190 mm  ->  p_static = 3.52 kPa
compaction/tamping factor 10x   ->  p_design = 35.2 kPa
```

Kirchhoff plate centre deflection of a simply-supported square panel,
`w = 0.00406 q a^4 / D`, PETG at E = 2.0 GPa, nu = 0.4:

| wall | vessel (100 mm span) | block (190 mm span) |
|---|---|---|
| 12 mm | 0.042 mm | — |
| 16 mm | 0.018 mm | 0.229 mm |
| **20 mm** | **0.009 mm** | **0.117 mm** |
| 25 mm | 0.005 mm | 0.060 mm |
| 30 mm | — | 0.035 mm |

The tolerance target is 0.10 mm, because a mould that bulges casts an out-of-tolerance
wall thickness, and wall thickness is the single parameter the drying and oxygen
models are most sensitive to. The vessel clears it with an order of magnitude to
spare; the block at 20 mm sits at 0.117 mm, marginally over. That is accepted rather
than thickened because the block's flange and the six clamping bolts restrain the
panel mid-span, which the simply-supported idealisation ignores — but if cast blocks
come out thick at mid-face, **the block mould wall is the thing to increase** (25 mm
gives 0.060 mm).

Realised minimum wall, measured from the cavity surface to the outside of the block:

| | minimum | median |
|---|---|---|
| vessel lower / upper | 19.57 / 18.75 mm | 23.75 / 24.01 mm |
| block lower / upper | 20.00 / 20.00 mm | 22.00 / 22.00 mm |

The vessel's 18.75 mm minimum is one voxel under nominal (1.25 mm pitch) where the
drafted cavity encroaches near the parting flange — within discretisation, not a
design thin spot.

## 6. Verification

### Volume balance — attributed, not hand-tallied

The check is `residual = block − lower − upper − cavity`, and the residual is then
**attributed** by intersecting it with each named feature. Anything the named
features cannot explain lands in `unattributed`, which is the number that actually
catches a mis-signed boolean or a feature that cut through a wall. A hand-written
tally cannot do this, because a wrong boolean changes both sides of the tally at once.

| quantity | vessel | block |
|---|---|---|
| mould block | 3 379 068.4 mm³ | 24 864 768.0 mm³ |
| lower half | 1 046 158.2 | 8 618 208.0 |
| upper half | 1 440 496.1 | 8 552 960.0 |
| cavity (cast body + draft) | 854 962.9 | 7 566 784.0 |
| residual void | 37 451.2 | 126 816.0 |
| — drains | 3 890.6 | 29 696.0 |
| — clamping bolts | 8 439.5 | 21 120.0 |
| — feed/vent gates | 24 777.3 | 75 264.0 |
| — key socket clearance | 343.8 | 736.0 |
| **unattributed** | **0.0** | **0.0** |
| sum of all parts | 3 379 068.4 | 24 864 768.0 |
| **closure residual** | **0.0 mm³ (0.0 %)** | **0.0 mm³ (0.0 %)** |
| overlap lower∩upper | 0.0 | 0.0 |
| overlap half∩cavity | 0.0 / 0.0 | 0.0 / 0.0 |
| material outside the block | 0.0 | 0.0 |

Exact closure is expected here and is *not* evidence of a suspiciously good result:
the partition is computed on occupancy, where a set partition is exact by
construction, so any non-zero value would be a coding error. Mesh volumes are
reported separately because those *do* carry marching-cubes error:

| | occupancy | mesh | difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| vessel block | 3 379 068.4 mm³ | 3 378 711.9 mm³ | −0.011 % |
| vessel halves (sum) | 2 486 654.3 | 2 485 003.9 | −0.066 % |
| block halves (sum) | 17 171 168.0 | 17 168 320.0 | −0.017 % |

| `mould_block_lower` | yes | yes | 1 | 8 617 325.0 | 14 | 14 |
| `mould_block_upper` | yes | yes | 1 | 8 550 995.0 | 6 | 6 |

Genus is checked against the *counted* through-holes (bolts + drains + gates), not
merely asserted non-zero: a genus that disagrees with the hole count means a feature
broke through a wall or two holes merged.

### Release — kinematic sweep, not visual inspection

A cavity can be watertight, correctly sized, and still impossible to open. Each part
is translated along its release direction and tested for interference at every step:

| motion | clears | first interference |
|---|---|---|
| vessel body out of the lower half (+z) | **yes** | none in 156 steps |
| vessel body out of the upper half (−z) | **yes** | none in 156 steps |
| lower loose core out of its own half | **yes** | none in 156 steps |
| upper loose core out of its own half | **yes** | none in 156 steps |
| block body out of the lower half (+z) | **yes** | none in 120 steps |
| block body out of the upper half (−z) | **yes** | none in 120 steps |

The loose cores are tested against **their own half**, which is the physical case —
in the split-mould workflow the halves are cast and cured separately, and that
separation is exactly what exposes the parting face to atmosphere. As a control, the
same cores do *not* clear the fully assembled vessel (both `False`), confirming the
test discriminates and that the cores genuinely require per-half extraction.

Other geometric checks: cavity contains the form (**True**, oversize 0.069 %); cores
lie inside the vessel's hollow (**True**, undersize 3 613 mm³ = the deliberate draft
taper); block cavity within the nominal body (**True**, as-cast 94.7 % of nominal =
the draft relief).

## 7. Print settings

**Material — PETG.** Chosen over PLA because the mould is in sustained contact with
an alkaline urea/CaCl₂ cementation solution at 30 °C for 28 days. PLA hydrolyses
under exactly those conditions (warm, wet, alkaline) and will craze over a 28-day
cure. PETG holds up, and its 80 °C service temperature covers a warm-room cure with
margin. ABS also works chemically but warps badly at these part sizes
(390 × 190 mm footprint), and a warped parting face leaks slurry.

**Layer height 0.25 mm, nozzle 0.6 mm.** The smallest intentional feature is the
0.30 mm key clearance; everything else is ≥ 6 mm. There is no fine detail to
resolve, and the print is large (block halves are ~8.6 × 10⁶ mm³ each), so a coarse
layer is the right trade. Do **not** go finer expecting a better cast surface — see
the liner note below.

**Walls: 5 perimeters (3.0 mm shell), 25 % gyroid infill, 6 top / 5 bottom layers.**
The 20 mm mould wall is not printed solid — 5 perimeters plus gyroid delivers the
plate stiffness the §5 calculation assumes at roughly a third of the material. Gyroid
specifically, because it is isotropic: the mix pushes normal to every cavity face, and
a directional infill (grid, triangles) is compliant on one axis. Increase to 40 % for
the block if mid-face bulge shows up in cast parts.

**Print orientation: parting face DOWN on the bed.** The parting face is the sealing
surface and the flattest reference; printing it against the glass gives the best
flatness and puts the layer lines perpendicular to the clamping load. This also
means the cavity overhangs print upward with the draft angle working in your favour
(2.5° / 1.5° of taper is self-supporting). Supports: none needed inside the cavity;
the drains and bolt holes are vertical.

**Release agent AND a liner — both, for different reasons.**

- **Liner (mandatory, not optional).** FDM layer lines are a mechanical key: fresh
  bio-cement penetrates the 0.25 mm grooves and locks. A demoulded green body will
  tear its surface off. Line the cavity with 0.05–0.1 mm PTFE film or a thin
  latex/silicone skin. This is also why a finer layer height is not the fix — the
  liner makes the printed surface finish irrelevant, so print coarse and fast.
- **Release agent (on top of the liner).** Light mould oil or a silicone spray on
  the liner. Avoid anything acidic; the MICP reaction is alkaline and pH-sensitive,
  and a fatty-acid release agent can locally suppress precipitation at the surface —
  which would show up as a soft skin on the cast face, not as a demoulding problem.

**Sealing the print.** PETG printed at 25 % infill is not watertight through the
wall. Brush the cavity side with two coats of epoxy before fitting the liner, or the
cementation solution will wick into the infill and the mould will weep. The liner
alone is not a vapour barrier at the seams.

**Clamping.** Bolt the flanges: **vessel 4 × M8 through Ø8.5 mm holes** on a
r = 89.5 mm ring; **block 6 × M10 through Ø10.5 mm holes** on the rectangular
perimeter. Use washers under both head and nut — PETG creeps under a point load over
28 days, and a bolt that relaxes opens the parting line. Torque hand-tight only: the
registration cones, not the bolts, set the alignment, and over-torquing the flange
bows the parting face and *opens* the cavity at mid-span. Add a 1–2 mm closed-cell
foam or silicone bead gasket in the flange outside the cavity to catch slurry weep at
the parting line. Re-check torque at 24 h once the mix has settled.

**Curing orientation.** Stand the vessel mould with the **parting plane horizontal
and the halves cast separately, open face up** — that open face is the atmosphere
contact that makes bio-cementation possible at all (oxygen reaches ~57 mm through
air-filled pores versus ~0.3 mm through water-filled ones). Do not assemble the two
halves until both bodies have cured; assembling early converts the parting face from
an oxygen source into a sealed interface and reproduces the paper's Fig. 5 failure.
The drains go at the **bottom** during cure so spent solution runs out rather than
pooling in the cast body's base and keeping it saturated.
